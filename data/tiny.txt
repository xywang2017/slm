The little robot lived in a quiet garden. Each morning it watched the sun rise over the trees.
The robot asked the gardener, "How do seeds become flowers?"
"With water, light, and time," said the gardener. "Small steps can make great changes."
The little robot carried water to the garden. It counted the leaves and listened to the birds.
One day a bird landed on its hand. The robot stayed still, and the bird began to sing.
"I cannot sing like you," said the robot, "but I can learn your song."
The gardener smiled. "Learning begins with listening."
At night the robot looked at the stars. It wondered how far their light had traveled.
The next morning it planted a seed. Every day it returned with water and a new question.
A green leaf appeared. Then another. Soon a yellow flower opened toward the sun.
The little robot had learned something: a patient friend can help a small thing grow.
The garden was full of questions. Why does rain fall? Where do birds go in winter?
The gardener did not know every answer. Together they read books and tried experiments.
They measured shadows at noon. They collected rain in a cup. They drew maps of the sky.
Sometimes the robot made a mistake. It wrote the mistake down and tried again.
"A mistake is useful when it teaches us," said the gardener.
The robot built a little shelter for the seeds. The birds brought songs to the garden.
In the evening the friends sat beneath the tree, watching the quiet world change.
